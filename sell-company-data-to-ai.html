How to sell your company's data to AI companies.

Five steps, in the order they actually happen. Most owners get stuck on the first one.

Step one

Start by proving you own the record.

This is where most deals die, and it dies quietly because owners assume the answer is yes.

A company holds two kinds of material. The first is its own operating record, which it owns outright. The second is material held on behalf of somebody else, usually a client under a master agreement that assigns ownership to them. The second kind cannot be licensed no matter how valuable it looks.

Sort this before anything else. Any serious buyer will ask for a clean provenance chain, and a seller who cannot produce one has nothing to sell.

Step two

Find what is actually in there.

Owners routinely misjudge their own record in both directions.

The systems that turn out to matter are rarely the ones people name first. Support tickets, internal threads, quote revisions, field notes, and call recordings tend to carry more of the reasoning than the CRM or the accounting export does.

The question to answer is not how many gigabytes exist. It is how many years of judgment are visible inside them.

Step three

Get it priced by someone who will write the cheque.

There is no public price list for this asset, and the comparables that circulate publicly come from large publishers licensing catalogs. They are the wrong reference point for an operating business.

Two routes exist. A company can run a direct procurement process with a frontier lab, which is slow and rarely open to businesses of ordinary size. Or it can license to a data lab that buys as a principal and already supplies those labs.

Polyshares is the second kind. We quote a number and we pay it. Nothing is contingent on us placing the data somewhere afterwards.

Step four

Paper a license, not a sale.

The instinct is to sell the data. The better structure is to license it.

A license grants defined rights over defined material for a defined period. The company keeps ownership, keeps operating on its own records, and can license again later. A sale ends all of that permanently in exchange for a single payment.

Scope and term are the two things worth reading closely. A bounded license should say exactly what material is covered and exactly what may be done with it.

Step five

Approve, share, get paid.

A buyer will want to see the record before committing to a number. Nobody pays for material they have not examined, and an owner should be suspicious of anyone who offers to.

Once the scope is approved and the data is shared, identifying fields are stripped as part of preparing it. At Polyshares that work is ours rather than yours, and it happens before anything moves.

Payment follows on net 30 to 60 terms.

Questions

Common questions about selling to AI companies.

How do I sell my company's data to AI companies?

Establish that you own the records, take an inventory of what the operating history actually contains, get it priced by someone who will write the cheque, paper a bounded license, then approve and share it. Going direct to a frontier lab means a procurement cycle measured in quarters. Selling to a data lab that already supplies them collapses that to weeks.

Do I need to approach the AI labs myself?

No, and for most companies it is the wrong route. The labs buy through a small number of data labs rather than opening a procurement process for every operating business. Polyshares is one of those data labs. We buy the license from you directly and handle everything downstream.

What do AI companies want from an ordinary business?

The working record rather than the polished output. Threads, tickets, drafts, revisions, and correspondence that show a problem being worked through. Finished reports carry the answer without the reasoning that produced it.

How long does the process take?

Terms are papered within a week of the first conversation. Payment is net 30 to 60 once the data has been approved, shared, and anonymized. A company running a direct procurement process with a frontier lab should expect materially longer.

What does it cost to start?

Nothing. Polyshares is the buyer, not an agent, so there is no fee and no commission at any stage. If we do not do a deal, nothing has changed for you.
