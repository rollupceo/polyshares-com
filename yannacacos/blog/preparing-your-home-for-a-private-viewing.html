Preparing Your Home for a Private Viewing

March 11, 2026 · 3 min read

A private viewing is different from an open house. The buyer has already seen the photos, already discussed the price, and is walking in looking for a reason to move forward or a reason to walk. You generally get one shot, often on short notice.

Light is the highest-leverage thing you control. Open every blind and curtain, and turn on every light in the house, including closets and the range hood, regardless of the time of day. Rooms photograph and show dramatically better lit than they do in natural light alone, and dim corners read as smaller.

Then handle smell, which people notice before they consciously register anything else. Take the trash out, run the disposal, and avoid cooking anything strong that day. Skip the heavy plug-ins and candles, though: an aggressively scented house reads as covering something up. Neutral and clean beats fragrant.

Clear the horizontal surfaces. Counters, the dining table, nightstands, and the area around the sink should be close to empty. This is not about tidiness for its own sake, it is that empty surfaces make rooms read as larger and let a buyer picture their own things there.

Set the temperature comfortably ahead of time, and in an Oklahoma summer, ahead of time means an hour. A house that is warm when a buyer walks in raises a question about the HVAC that you do not want raised.

Then leave, and take the pets with you. Buyers do not speak freely in front of sellers, and the conversation they have while walking through is where their real objections surface — objections your agent can only answer if the buyer feels free to say them out loud.

One last pass on the way out: toilet lids down, shower doors clean, pet bowls and litter boxes put away, and the front entry swept. The first ten feet set the tone for everything that follows.
