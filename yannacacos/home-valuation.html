What’s Your Property Worth?

Knowing what your home is worth turns a vague sense of your finances into something you can plan around — whether you are weighing a move, a renovation, or simply want an honest read on where you stand.

What you get here is a figure grounded in the local market rather than a national average. Michael reviews your property himself, which is the part the big listing portals cannot do for you.

What is a home valuation?

A valuation puts a number on what your property would realistically sell for right now. It reads recent nearby sales alongside the condition and character of your specific home, which makes it the starting point for almost any decision involving the equity you hold.

How is the valuation of my home calculated?

Location carries the most weight, followed by age, square footage, layout, condition, and any upgrades you have put in. Those get weighed against what comparable homes nearby have actually closed at, then adjusted for the direction the local market is currently moving.

How accurate is the online home valuation?

Portal estimates are a fair opening guess, but they work from public records and never see the inside of the house. Renovations, a premium lot, deferred maintenance, and street-level differences all go unrecorded, and those are usually where the real gap between an estimate and an offer sits.

How Is a Valuation Performed?

Two Accurate Ways to Perform Home Valuations

Market Analysis

Comparative Market Analysis

A comparative market analysis is assembled by an agent rather than generated by software. Michael pulls homes near yours that recently sold and are genuinely comparable in size, age, and finish — normally at least three — and then adjusts each one up or down for the ways it differs from your property.

Because it is prepared by hand by someone who has walked these streets, a CMA can account for the things a database cannot: how the kitchen actually presents, which side of the block is quieter, and how quickly similar listings have been moving.

Appraisals

Based on a Professional’s Opinion

An appraisal is an independent opinion of value produced by a licensed appraiser, most often ordered by a lender once financing is in play. It carries no stake in the outcome of the sale, which is exactly why lenders rely on it.

The appraiser inspects the property in person and delivers a formal written report. Expect to pay for the service — typically up to around $500 — and expect the timeline to run longer than a CMA, since scheduling and reporting are both part of the process.

Why Is a Valuation Important?

Situations When a Home Valuation May Be Necessary

Refinancing

Lenders size a refinance against your property value, so knowing where you stand tells you what is actually available to you before you apply.

Home Improvements

A valuation helps you judge whether a planned improvement will return its cost, or whether it would overprice your home for the neighborhood.

Qualifying for Credit

Most lenders want to see meaningful equity — commonly at least 20% — before extending a home equity line of credit.

Planning

Whether or not you are selling soon, knowing your home value is a real input into any longer-term financial plan.
