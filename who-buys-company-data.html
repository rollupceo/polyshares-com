Who buys company data.

The chain is shorter than most owners assume, and the company at the end of it is not the one you sign with.

The chain, end to end.

Frontier labs training general purpose models sit at one end. They need operating material at scale and their standard for provenance is the strictest in the market. Applied AI companies building for a single industry sit alongside them, and for a specialized record they often want it more badly.

Neither group is set up to negotiate with a fifty person business in a specialized trade. Opening procurement against thousands of individual companies is not a process any of them run.

So the buying happens through data labs. A data lab sources the material, verifies that the seller has the right to license it, clears and prepares it, and supplies the labs. Polyshares is a data lab, and our focus is enterprise operating records rather than anything produced to order.

What that means for the company selling.

You are not being introduced to anyone. You are not being listed. There is no auction and no queue.

You sign one license, with us, and we pay you. What happens downstream is our problem and our risk. If we misjudge what the record is worth, that is our loss rather than a renegotiation with you.

This is the practical difference between a buyer and an agent. An agent is paid out of your proceeds and carries none of the exposure.

What any buyer checks first.

Provenance. Nobody with regulatory exposure signs without a clean chain showing the seller had the right to grant the license. This is checked before price is discussed, and it is the most common reason a promising record goes nowhere.

Then whether personal information can be removed properly, then how much of the record is genuinely unpublished. Material scraped from the open web has been free for years.

Questions

Questions about buyers.

Who buys company operational data?

In practice, data labs do. Frontier labs and applied AI companies need the material but rarely run procurement against individual operating businesses, so they buy through a small number of firms that source, clear, and prepare it. Polyshares is one of those firms, and when we license your record we are the buyer on the contract.

Can I sell straight to a frontier lab?

A large publisher with a famous catalog can. An operating business of ordinary size usually cannot get a procurement process opened at all, and the ones that do are measuring the timeline in quarters.

Will my competitors be able to buy my data?

No. Scope and permitted use are written into the license, and we control where the material goes. A company that lists on an open marketplace gives up that control entirely.

Do buyers want data from small companies?

Yes, when the record is deep and specialized. Nobody is indexing by company size. What is scarce is operating history in domains where written records are thin, and small companies in specialized trades often hold exactly that.

Does Polyshares disclose which labs receive the data?

No. Downstream relationships and deal-level terms stay private on both sides.

Can I license to more than one buyer?

That depends on the license you sign. A bounded, non-exclusive structure leaves the company free to license the same record again as it grows.
