Who buys company data.

The buyer set is smaller than the headlines suggest, and it is not the group most owners picture.

Three kinds of buyer.

Frontier labs training general purpose models are the buyers everyone has heard of. They buy broadly and they buy at scale, and their standard for provenance is the strictest in the market.

Applied AI companies building for one industry are the quieter half of the market, and for a specialized record they frequently pay more. A company building a product for a particular trade needs the operating history of that trade, and there is no substitute for it.

Data aggregators assemble collections and license them onward. They move faster and pay less, and a seller working through one gives up visibility into where the material ends up.

What every buyer checks first.

Provenance. A buyer with regulatory exposure will not sign without a clean chain showing the seller had the right to grant the license. This is checked before price is discussed, and it is the most common reason a promising record goes nowhere.

Then whether personal information has been removed properly, then how much of the record is genuinely unpublished. Material that has been scraped from the open web has been free to them for years.

Why the seller should not be shopping itself.

An operating company approaching buyers directly is negotiating once against a counterparty that negotiates constantly. It has no view of current pricing and no alternative bid to bargain against.

It also spends months on qualification before any conversation about price begins. Under a represented mandate that work has already been done, which is most of why the timeline compresses to weeks.

Questions

Questions about buyers.

Who buys company operational data?

Three groups. Frontier AI labs training general models, applied AI companies building products for a specific industry, and data aggregators assembling collections to license onward. The second group often pays the most for a narrow record, because a specialized operating history is exactly what a vertical product needs.

Will my competitors be able to buy my data?

Not under a Polyshares mandate. Scope and permitted use are written into the license, and the buyer set is ours to control. A seller who lists on an open marketplace gives up that control.

Do buyers want data from small companies?

Yes, when the record is deep and specialized. Buyers are not indexing by company size. They are looking for operating history in domains where written records are scarce, and small companies in specialized trades often hold exactly that.

Does Polyshares publish who the buyers are?

No. Buyer relationships and deal-level terms stay private, which is a condition of running a credible process for both sides.

Can I license to more than one buyer?

Yes, unless the license is exclusive. A non-exclusive structure allows the same record to be licensed again, which is often worth more over time than a single exclusive payment.
