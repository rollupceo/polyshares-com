Data licensing

Sell your company's operational data.

Polyshares represents companies licensing their operating record to the buyers paying for it. We price the asset, hold the buyer relationships, and take our fee from the proceeds.

Most companies hold an asset they have never priced.

Every business with real operating history has produced a second asset without meaning to. It is the record of how the work actually got done, accumulated year after year as a byproduct of running.

The AI labs need that material and are paying for it. The market is young, and most owners have never priced what they hold because until recently there was nothing to price it against.

Polyshares works for the seller. We price the asset, take it to the buyers, and negotiate the license. The company keeps its equity and keeps its data.

Operational data means the working record of the business. Slack and Teams, Drive and SharePoint, email, CRM history, support tickets, design files, call recordings, accounting and ERP exports.

What buyers are actually paying for.

The value is not in volume. It is in reasoning that was captured while the work happened.

A support ticket that shows a problem being misdiagnosed, argued over, and finally resolved is worth more than a clean table of finished numbers. A thread where two people disagree about a quote and work it out carries judgment that cannot be synthesized. The messy working record is the point, not a defect in it.

This is why an ordinary operating business can hold something a data vendor cannot manufacture. Nobody can fabricate ten years of a company arguing with itself and getting to an answer.

What Polyshares is not.

We are not a marketplace and we will not list your company on one. Listings put a seller in a queue behind everyone else and reward whoever uploaded the most.

We represent one seller at a time and take the asset to buyers directly. You are not bidding against the other companies we work with, and your material is never pooled with theirs.

We also acquire lower middle market businesses outright, which is a separate mandate. An owner thinking about selling the company rather than licensing its record can approach us on that basis instead.

Questions

Questions owners ask before the first call.

Can a company sell its operational data?

In most cases what changes hands is a license, not the data itself. The company keeps ownership and keeps using its own records exactly as before. The buyer receives defined rights, for a defined period, over a defined body of material.

How much is company operational data worth?

Polyshares deals typically land between $100K and $1M paid to the company. Depth of operating history and how much visible reasoning the record contains move the number far more than raw volume does.

Does a data license dilute the company?

No. A license carries no equity and no debt. It does not touch the cap table, and it creates no repayment obligation.

What does Polyshares charge?

Our fee comes out of the proceeds of the deal. If nothing sells, you owe us nothing.

How long does a data licensing deal take?

Terms are agreed and papered within a week of the first conversation. Money reaches the company account in eight to twelve weeks, once the buyer closes.

Does customer information leave the company?

Anonymization happens before anything leaves your systems, and handling it is our responsibility rather than yours. Identifying material is stripped as part of preparing the asset.

What kind of company qualifies?

Any business with real operating history. Venture backed, middle market, or founder owned. The length and texture of the record matter more than headcount or revenue.
