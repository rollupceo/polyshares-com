How a data licensing deal works.

From the first conversation to money in the company account, and what happens on each side.

The sequence.

The structure does not change between deals. Only the asset and the price do.

Day one
A five question intake, then a call. You learn whether buyers exist for a record of your shape.
Within a week
Terms agreed and the mandate papered. Scope, fee, and term are written down before any work begins.
Weeks one to three
We inventory the record and prepare the asset. Anonymization happens here, inside your systems, before anything leaves.
Weeks three to eight
We take the asset to buyers and negotiate. You take no meetings.
Weeks eight to twelve
The buyer closes and funds reach the company account.
Your obligations
A call, a decision, and access to the systems being licensed. Nothing else.
If nothing sells
You owe us nothing. The fee comes out of the proceeds.

Why it takes eight to twelve weeks.

Almost none of that time is negotiation. It is buyer diligence.

A buyer with regulatory exposure verifies the provenance chain before it discusses price, and that verification is slow by design. A company running the process alone usually adds months in front of this, spent finding and qualifying buyers before any of it starts.

The compressed timeline comes from the buyer relationships already existing. It does not come from anyone hurrying diligence.

What we need from you.

Confirmation that the company owns the records, which is the one step that has to start on your side because only you hold your customer contracts.

Access to the systems being licensed, scoped to what the mandate covers.

A decision on scope and exclusivity when an offer is on the table. Everything else sits with us.

Questions

Questions about the process.

How does a data licensing deal work?

At Polyshares: a five question intake, then a call, then a mandate papered within a week. We prepare and anonymize the asset, take it to buyers, and negotiate. Money reaches the company account eight to twelve weeks after the mandate, on the buyer closing.

How much of my time does it take?

A call, a decision on the mandate, and access to the systems being licensed. You take no buyer meetings. Preparing the asset, running the process, and negotiating are ours.

When do I know whether there is a market for my record?

Quickly. The intake and one conversation are enough for us to say whether buyers exist for a record of that shape. A company that does not qualify is told so rather than left waiting.

What if nothing sells?

You owe nothing. The fee is taken from the proceeds, so a deal that does not close costs the company nothing but the call.

Can I stop the process once it has started?

The mandate defines the term and how it ends. This is agreed in writing at the outset rather than left informal.
